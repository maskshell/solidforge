## 6.x Retrospective evidence from production runs (DRAFT)

The framework above predicts two observable signatures in a production convergence loop that runs both a same-source and a heterogeneously-decoupled review leg: (i) the two legs' discoveries should substantially diverge (each oracle's blind-spot set differs, §4.4); (ii) a fresh-context reviewer should catch defects that the loop's own earlier fixes introduce — the maker-checker asymmetry of §3.2(iii). We test neither causally; we report what the production record corpus shows, with its limitations stated.

**Corpus.** 46 convergence-run units carrying 797 findings from their authoritative aggregate records (the naive per-file count at first harvest was 1,461 — the difference is the corpus's documented round-file/aggregate duplication, resolved by the unit rules), from the reference implementation's own doc-convergence runs and five consumer codebases, harvested read-only from durable run records (one author-maintained evidence corpus; consumer projects pseudonymized). Findings are UNADJUDICATED reviewer claims — counts are claim-counts, not confirmed-defect counts; severity strata are reported separately and honest-disclosure items are excluded from defect denominators.

**Signature (i) — leg divergence.** Among the 359 defect-severity findings whose producing leg is attributable from surviving per-leg records, the same-source leg alone discovered 192 (53.5%) and the heterogeneous leg alone 167 (46.5%). Within this attribution coverage (54% of defect findings; the remainder sit in units without per-leg records), each leg alone would have lost roughly half of what the pair recovered. This is the retrospective analogue of the divergence observation in Open Problem 8 — not the known-gap test itself: without ground truth on which findings are true defects, divergence here measures discovery complementarity, not verdict correctness. Both-report duplication cannot be measured at id level (cross-leg identifiers never coincide); a kind-and-location pair adjudication would move findings from single-leg buckets into the both-bucket and cannot remove them, so the single-leg loss estimate is an upper bound.

**Signature (ii) — fix-introduced defects.** An LLM-assisted coding pass over fix-vocabulary-filtered candidate findings (319 of 667 multi-round defect findings), spot-checked against record full text with zero disagreement (5/5 positives, 3 negatives drawn, 2 effective after one unverifiable lookup-miss (docs/coding-pass-verification-protocol.md rule 1); single-seat limitation disclosed), with a second independent seat re-adjudicating a 20% subsample at 96.9% strict-match agreement (zero genuinely disputed positives), classifies 23 findings (3.4% of all multi-round defect findings; 7.2% of the enriched candidate pool) as defects explicitly attributed by the finding itself to an EARLIER FIX in the same run — residues of incomplete class-fixes, overshoots, and rewording regressions. The pattern is pervasive across runs and engines (positives recorded in five of the ten units the candidate pool spans) rather than an artifact of any single integration. Every such finding was caught within the loop, by a reviewer context that did not author the fix under review. The causal reading — "caught only because the reviewer was fresh" — is a counterfactual this corpus cannot carry and we do not claim it.

**Limitations.** No control baseline exists (nothing here compares the loop against single-pass review); environment provenance is recoverable for a minority of units, so results are not stratified by model era; the corpus is a survivorship-biased snapshot (degraded and unrecorded runs are underrepresented); the coding pass is single-seat with a consistency re-run reported alongside. These bound the section's claim to: in this production corpus, the two oracle legs' discoveries are near-symmetric complements, and fix-introduced defects are a recurring, catchable class — consistent with, not proof of, the two-axis frame's predictions.
